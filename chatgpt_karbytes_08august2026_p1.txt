3. It may generate opaque internal reasoning state that helps it decompose the problem, compare possibilities, or decide whether to use a tool.

4. It selects a textual response, tool call, or other action.

5. The surrounding Work system performs the permitted action and returns the result.

6. The model processes the enlarged context, revises its plan, and continues until it reaches an answer or stopping condition.

GPT‑5.6 can reuse compatible reasoning items across turns, but OpenAI describes that as *persisted reasoning*, not as a continuous private stream of consciousness. ## What happens inside a chip?

At a simplified level:

1. A power supply establishes electric potentials across the hardware.

2. Electric fields exert forces on charge carriers.

3. Transistors switch, repeatedly charging and discharging tiny capacitances.

4. Those switching patterns implement numerical operations on the model’s activations and parameters.

5. Electrical energy is partly stored momentarily in fields and largely converted into thermal motion.

6. Fans, pumps, air, or coolant move the resulting heat out of the hardware.

For one capacitive node, the energy temporarily stored when charged to voltage (V) is approximately

[preformatted_text]

// ChatGPT's LaTeX output
[
E_{\text{stored}}=\frac{1}{2}CV^2.
]

// karbytes' plain-text conversion of ChatGPT's LaTeX output
[
E_stored = 0.5 * C * V * V
]

[end preformatted_text]

Digital processors repeat such charging and discharging processes across enormous numbers of nodes. Leakage currents and memory movement also consume energy. Most of that energy eventually becomes heat.

It would nevertheless be misleading to imagine individual electrons carrying particular words from memory into the response. A token such as “Thinking” is a macroscopic interpretation of a distributed physical configuration. The semantic pattern persists while the particular charge carriers involved continually change.

## Does mass have to be displaced?

No. The definition of physical work concerns **force acting through displacement**, not necessarily a macroscopic massive object being transported from one location to another.

Electric fields can perform work on charged particles through microscopic displacements. Electromagnetic radiation can transfer energy and momentum despite photons having no invariant rest mass. A fan can consume positive work over many revolutions even though each point on a blade repeatedly returns to its starting position; work depends on the complete path integral, not merely the final minus initial position.

The servers themselves also move with Earth’s rotation and orbit, but that motion is not meaningfully attributable to the chatbot’s computation. The task-specific mechanical displacements are primarily microscopic charge motion, atomic vibration, fan and pump rotation, and coolant or airflow.

So the most precise synthesis is:

> **In the interface, “work” means elapsed task-directed computation and coordination. In physics, that computation is realized through electromagnetic energy transfer, microscopic charge displacement, transistor switching, thermal vibration, and cooling-system motion. The former is reported in seconds; the latter would have to be measured in joules, and the interface does not provide enough information to calculate it.**

### Mechanical work

[preformatted_text]

[
W=\int_{\gamma}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r

* (d\mathbf r): infinitesimal spatial-displacement vector of the force’s point of application, measured in meters ((\mathrm m)).

[preformatted_text]

[
W=Fd
]

[end preformatted_text]

* (F): magnitude of the constant force, in newtons.

* (d): distance traveled in the force’s direction, in meters.

* (W): work, with (\mathrm{N,m}=\mathrm J).

### Electrical work and energy

[preformatted_text]

[
W_{\text{electrical}}=\int V(t)I(t),dt
=\int P(t),dt
]

[end preformatted_text]

* (W_{\text{electrical}}): electrical energy transferred or electrical work performed, in joules.

* (t): time, in seconds ((\mathrm s)).

* (dt): infinitesimal time interval.

* (V(t)): voltage, or electric potential difference, at time (t), in volts ((\mathrm V)).

* (I(t)): electric current at time (t), in amperes ((\mathrm A)).

* (P(t)): electrical power at time (t), in watts ((\mathrm W)), where (P(t)=V(t)I(t)).

* (\int): accumulation of the instantaneous power over the relevant time interval.

### Energy attributable to a task

[preformatted_text]

[

]
