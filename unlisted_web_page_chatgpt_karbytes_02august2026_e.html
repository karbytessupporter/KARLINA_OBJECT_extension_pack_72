As a technical aside, I noticed that the original PNG version of the JPG image I shared with you in this conversation was approximately 13 megabytes while the JPG version was only approximately 4 megabytes. What accounts for that file size discrepancy?

ChatGPT:

The discrepancy is primarily because PNG is lossless while JPEG is lossy.

PNG must preserve every pixel exactly. Its compression works especially well on flat colors and repeated patterns, but your screenshot contains numerous photographic previews—foliage, clouds, grass, hills, shadows, and image noise. Those contain large amounts of irregular pixel-level variation that PNG cannot discard, so they compress relatively poorly.

JPEG reduces the data much more aggressively:

* It divides the image into small blocks and represents them as spatial-frequency information.

* It quantizes that information, discarding subtle details the encoder predicts will be difficult to notice.

* It commonly reduces color resolution through chroma subsampling.

* It compresses smooth gradients and photographic textures much more efficiently than PNG.

The Bluesky interface’s flat backgrounds and text favor PNG, but its many large photographs favor JPEG strongly. A reduction from about 13 MB to 4 MB—roughly 3.25×—is therefore quite plausible.

Other possible contributors include:

* The PNG may contain an alpha channel, while JPEG cannot store transparency.

* The conversion may have reduced the pixel dimensions.

* JPEG quality settings may have been moderately aggressive.

* Metadata may differ, although metadata usually explains only a small fraction of a discrepancy that large.

The tradeoff is that JPEG may introduce slight blurring, ringing around text, color bleeding, and block artifacts. PNG retains perfectly sharp text and UI boundaries.

Incidentally, the copy delivered to me was a platform-generated derivative measuring only **92 × 2,048 pixels and approximately 85 KB**. It was evidently downscaled and recompressed substantially beyond your approximately 4 MB local JPEG. That explains why I could identify the post cards and photographs but could not reliably read most of their tiny text.
