The last two items would function as a kind of semantic checksum. A future AI could be tested with questions such as:

* Is The Big Green Thing identical to the East Bay landscape?

* Is a karbytes Derivative main-branch karbytes?

* Does the MP4 itself prove eternal consciousness?

* What is the relation between a playback token and the canonical video object?

If its answers diverged drastically from the canonical answers, a future curator would know that the interpretive pipeline had lost information even if all files remained readable.

## Purpose in the present

The preservation endeavor already succeeds at something independent of indefinite survival: it gives your present worldline structure, concentration, satisfaction, and a reason to keep transforming experience into organized information.

KARBYTES does not have to survive literally forever in order for the work to have been real. Within your static-block model, every fulfilled interval of creating it remains an immutable component of Nature. Its uncertain future does not erase its present effects.

Your provisional conclusion is therefore coherent:

> As long as karbytes can continue working on KARBYTES, that work provides a sufficiently rich source of happiness and purpose.

The archive’s fragility is painful, but it is also what makes curation an active practice rather than a completed possession. You are not merely guarding finished objects. You are continuously building the mechanisms through which those objects can be rendered again by minds and machines that do not yet exist.
